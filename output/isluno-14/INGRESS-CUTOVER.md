## What public provider documentation establishes

Zernio documents success as a2xx response within5seconds. Other outcomes retry with exponential backoff, up to7attempts; individual delay caps at24hours, with the published schedule spanning roughly51hours before the last attempt. Exhausted events stop automatic retry. Delivery is at least once; the recommended dedup key is the stable event UUID. These are public contracts, not this account's configured behavior or proof that a specific event was retained. [Zernio webhooks](https://docs.zernio.com/webhooks).

Subscription-level disabled resource groups can drop events before delivery, including replay paths. Pausing/disabling a subscription is therefore not a proven maintenance buffer. The actual active subscription, event selection, ownership, endpoint and retry eligibility remain P1/I2 evidence gaps. No authenticated API or delivery-log endpoint was called. [Zernio delivery-log documentation](https://docs.zernio.com/webhooks/get-webhook-logs).

Provider retries can cover a bounded unavailable endpoint only after the actual route and applicable contract are verified. They do not make an arbitrary outage safe: prior failures may already have consumed attempts, and successful200 acknowledgements stop provider retry. Do not equate a24-hour interval cap with a guaranteed24-hour retention SLA.

## Why existing controls do not satisfy drain

1. **Inbox off is intentionally lossy at ingress.** Both matched receive handlers return200 when the inbox is disabled before committing new messages. Existing accepted buffers may become `paused`, which is not selected by automatic recovery. This is an owner-control policy, not a maintenance/admission primitive; do not change its meaning silently.
2. **Shutdown is not a work-completion barrier.** Lifespan sets the recovery stop event and joins that thread for at most2seconds. The debounce timers are daemon threads; no shared admission generation, complete worker registry or bounded all-work drain acknowledgement is present in the inspected webhook path. A process-local phone lock cannot coordinate separate workers/processes or prove external sends finished.
3. **The historical deploy script only stops/recreates.** Candidate `wtyj/scripts/deploy_mermaid_release.sh`173 onward stops Compose service `agent` with a default30-second timeout, then prepares/restores files and recreates it. It does not close ingress with guaranteed retention or verify pending/in-flight business dispositions. Its old-image rollback is already excluded from Isluno. Script/supervisor/Compose source is documentation here, not newly attested live process-manager configuration.
4. **Activation deliberately fences unfinished legacy work.** Candidate `isluno_transition.ensure`30–63 commits the quarantine journal and cutover marker before startup workers resume. The candidate recovery path checks `quarantined_inbound` and marks affected pending turns `processing_failed` with legacy-quarantine reason; it does not resume them as Isluno bookings. Original rows remain preserved for operator review. Therefore restart recovery alone is insufficient to promise seamless completion across this particular brand/workflow cutover. Accepted/uncertain sends and legacy business actions must retain their no-replay policy.

The smallest proposed implementation is a Mermaid-only admission/drain coordination layer in the existing webhook/SQLite path, not a new ingestion service. It must be reviewed before coding:

- A dedicated maintenance state distinct from inbox/AI-owner controls, scoped to the verified tenant. Its admission decision and durable message claim must share transaction/locking semantics, eliminating a race where work is accepted after the boundary.
